Round 12: consolidation. Two threads are made one by replay, never by merge, as [threads are consolidated, never merged](docs/decisions/threads-are-consolidated-never-merged.md) says; the spec is [consolidation](docs/specs/consolidation.md). The declaration gains `from`, a list of **seals**, `{ first, last }`: a source's first line id and its head, its last line in reading order (the decision said «the last line taken»; a source usually ends on a line the engine wrote and the replay does not take, so a seal that skipped it could never be compared with the head, and the decision's text is amended). `stamp`'s trailing arguments become one object, `stamp(draft, lines, now, opening?: { schema?, nonce?, from? })`, since all three matter only on an empty thread; `Io.send` receives `opening?: { nonce?, from? }` in place of the nonce; `post`, `dryRun` and `playScenario` take `from` beside `nonce`. `from` is never written absent as a key or empty, so a thread that names no source keeps its ids, which a test pins with two ids computed before the change. `replayed(lines, schema)` gives the lines a replay takes, every line the engine would not write again (all but a goal's lines and the records), the revoked, the outside and the found lines included, which `prune` leaves out for a template; both share one test of what the engine writes. `stepsOf` takes an optional `prefix` of every label, so two sources' labels stay apart. `schemaJson(schema)` is the inverse of `readSchema`, the schema without its kit. `deriveSchema(first, second, { answers, name })` derives the schema of the new thread: the same schema twice is itself, otherwise the parts are compared by canonical form (`opening`, `authority`, each record role, guard and gist are conflicts when they differ, and so is a law at two strengths; `sets` and `judged` are unions; gists, guards and record roles only one source has are added unasked) and a law only one source has is a conflict of its own, `one-sided-law`, answered `keep` or `drop`, since the new thread applies one set of laws, chosen by the person, as consolidated accounts apply uniform policies; an answer that does not fit its conflict is a problem; the host names the result, at version 1, with the first source's kit; the conflicts are listed in a fixed order whatever the answers. `consolidate(first, second)` refuses a source that is empty, not one chain or declared for another schema, and gives the derived schema, the steps (the first source's then the second's, labels prefixed `1:` and `2:`) and the two seals. `startDrill` and `stepDrill` walk the replay one step per call as a plain value: they stop at a schema conflict (first, second, by hand, abort) and at a step the new thread refuses or whose placeholder finds no line (again, edit, skip, abort), `again` working against a state the host has answered the cause in. Not solved, recorded in the spec: a reference to an engine line is a `$find` of its gist and payload, which in a consolidation may land on the other source's identical line. Round 11's callers and tests of `stamp` and `Io.send` were changed mechanically to the new objects. 599 tests in `consequitur` (from 493), 30 in `consequitur-kdl`, 71 in `consequitur-render`, 28 in the playground.

Round 11: the first line declares the thread. A thread names its schema and cannot repeat another, as [the first line declares the thread](docs/decisions/the-first-line-declares-the-thread.md) says. A schema may name itself: its head takes `name`, in lower kebab case, and `version`, a whole number from 1, both or neither, and `readSchema` lists a problem for one without the other or for either in the wrong form; `consequitur-kdl` reads and writes `name` and `version` as the first two nodes. A line has one field more, `thread`, the **declaration**, on a thread's first line only: a nonce, and the schema's name and version when it has them. `stamp` takes the schema and a nonce as two optional last arguments and writes `thread` when the thread is empty, with the nonce handed or 16 random bytes it draws in lowercase hex: the first randomness in the engine, and the reading still draws none. On that line the hash covers `thread` as a sixth field after `payload`, and the recipe keeps its name, `sha256`; every other line hashes its five fields, so no other id changes shape, and `verify` follows the same rule. `post`, `dryRun` and `playScenario` take an optional `nonce` and pass it on (`Io.send` gets it as a third argument), so a test or a scenario played twice gives the same ids. `inOrder`, given the schema, refuses a thread whose first line declares another schema, as it refuses a broken chain: it throws an `Error` whose sentence names both, absent counting as a value, so a first line with no declaration is read only by a schema with no name, and an empty thread by any. Every entry that reads a thread against a schema passes it the schema: `expectations`, and so `consequitur`, `offers`, `whenHolds`, `settle`, `dryRun` and `post`; `admit`, which now reads the thread's expectations once at its start; `prune`; and `lineFacts` in `consequitur-render`. The three cases are named after their folders, at version 1. The tests' helper that stamps a thread by hand stamps it for the schema that reads it, with one nonce, since the case now has a name and a drawn nonce would make two builds two threads; the test of a schema read as empty expects the refusal, as the empty schema has no name. 493 tests in `consequitur` (from 479), 30 in `consequitur-kdl` (from 24), 71 in `consequitur-render` (from 70), 28 in the playground.

Round 10: the id names its recipe. A line's id begins with the name of the recipe that made it, in the OCI digest form `algorithm:encoded`, as [the id names its recipe](docs/decisions/the-id-names-its-recipe.md) says. The only recipe is `sha256`: the five fields, in RFC 8785's form, as UTF-8, by SHA-256, and what is hashed does not change, so `stamp` writes `sha256:` before the same 64 hex characters, and `previous` quotes the id whole. `verify` reads the name before the first colon and checks the recipe it names; an id whose name it does not know, or that has none, does not verify, as an id that does not match its fields, and `verify` does not throw on a stored id that is malformed. Every id is seven characters longer, in the line and in the next line's `previous`. The test that pinned an id's pattern pins the name; new tests cover an unknown name, no name, a right name over wrong hex, and a JSON round trip. No thread is stored yet, so nothing migrates. 479 tests in `consequitur` (from 478), 24 in `consequitur-kdl`, 70 in `consequitur-render`, 28 in the playground.

The canonical form is RFC 8785. `canonical`, which the hash is taken of, was already the JSON Canonicalization Scheme for every value it allows, and its own examples are now tests (the sample object, the order of keys by UTF-16 code unit, the numbers of its appendix B). Where the scheme refuses, consequitur now refuses too, instead of hashing something else: `asStored` throws a `TypeError` on a number that is not finite, which JSON used to write as `null` so that a NaN in a draft was stored as `null` without a word, and on a string or a key that is not well-formed Unicode (a lone surrogate). So `stamp` throws on such a payload, and `admit` refuses it as `invalid-payload`; a writer that is not well-formed is refused as `writer-not-allowed`. Nothing stored changes and no id moves: a stored line never held NaN, and `verify` reads what it reads. 478 tests in `consequitur` (from 469), 24 in `consequitur-kdl`, 70 in `consequitur-render`, 28 in the playground.

Round 9: chain order. The reading order is the chain, as [the chain is the order](docs/decisions/the-chain-is-the-order.md) says. `inOrder` takes the first line to be the one whose `previous` is null and each next line the one that names the last, in one pass over a map from `previous` to line; the array's own order is still never read. A thread that is not one chain is refused, by `inOrder` alone, so every caller inherits it: it throws an `Error` whose sentence names the place, the last line read before the break, for a thread with no first line or two, a fork (two lines naming one `previous`), a gap (a `previous` that is not in the thread) or a line the walk never reaches; an empty thread is an empty chain. `stamp` writes the instant it is given and no longer bends it to the last timestamp plus a millisecond, so a timestamp may repeat or go back; `previous` is still the last line in reading order, and the hash covers what it covered. Nothing stored changes: a thread stamped with the old rule has its chain and its timestamps in one order, reads the same, and no id moves. `stepsOf` reads the lines kept in the order of the whole thread, since a part of a chain is no chain. The tests that pinned the bent millisecond and the ties by id are tests of the walk and of each way a thread breaks; the fixtures that built lines by hand are chains. 469 tests in `consequitur` (from 461), 24 in `consequitur-kdl`, 70 in `consequitur-render`, 28 in the playground.

Round 8: holes. A reference, `{ "$value": key }`, may stand wherever a JSON value stands in a payload, a list's item included; `$set`, `$writer` and `$draft` stay in conditions. The reading's second step runs in two passes over the lines in force: the lines whose payload holds no reference state their values, then every line is read with each reference replaced by that value, a reference to a key nothing stated left out (a key of an object, an item of a list), and that resolved payload is what every rule's `expect`, every `line` node of a condition and a `when` read; a line as stored keeps its reference, and its id covers it. A value stated through a reference serves no other, so there are no chains and no circles, and where no line holds a reference the two passes are one. One pure helper does it, `resolvePayload` in `check.ts` (with `referencesOf`), and `resolveCheck` reads the same one. `Reading.holes` lists each key a line in force refers to, a goal's condition and `with` included, and the first pass did not state, by key, with the lines that hold it: awaited, never denounced, so the thread stays settled; `changes` in `consequitur-render` says one `opened` or `filled`. The `value` rule gains its fill, `{ keyFrom, from, where? }`, which states the value under `from` under the key the payload holds under `keyFrom`, later line winning (`required` is not allowed with it); admission refuses a fill, as `invalid-payload`, whose key is not a string, is one some binding of the schema states, or whose value holds a reference. Admission reads a hole as present, so `required` is satisfied by it, and checks a rule's shape (`join`) on a reference only where it resolved: `RuleKind.admit` takes the payload with each reference that resolves replaced and the others kept, the payload as written and the schema. A `when` reads the draft with its references replaced. A goal's `with` may hold a reference: the outcome is written as the goal carries it, and completes when the key is stated. `holed(steps, holes)` in the package makes each payload value an application names a hole. The warehouse case gains the gist `fill`; «Saved» has holes for the supplier's reference, the unloader and the sorter and ends with their three fills, a dry run against «Tuesday» fills before it feeds the awaited lines and ends with no difference in the handles but those three keys, and «Second assignee» is a memo with `{ "$value": "legal" }` among its assignees, a goal on `has` of it, and a fill by a member of the team that makes the goal fire; its `schema.kdl` is written again with `toKdl`. The stopping law is not built: it is not in the base kit until a schema asks. 461 tests in `consequitur` (from 430), 24 in `consequitur-kdl`, 70 in `consequitur-render` (from 69), 28 in the playground.

Round 7: conditions. A gist's `when`, a goal's condition and `keeps-set`'s `while` are one language, kdlet's guards (`is`, `has`, `not`, `all`, `any`, a named guard) with two words consequitur registers, `line` (a line in force matches a check) and `atLeast`, written as JSON with references: `$value` and `$set` everywhere, `$writer` and `$draft` in a `when` only. The engine has one runtime dependency, `kdlet-guards` 0.2, imported by the new `condition.ts` alone, which reads a condition with the reader's strictness, translates it to the module's form for one evaluation and back (`toModuleForm`, `fromModuleForm`), and reads its trace for what can no longer hold. The schema's head names guards, `guards: { name: condition }`, used by `{ "guard": name }`; one that names a guard the head lacks, or depends on itself, is a problem. Admission reads `when` with the payload as it will be stored; the menu has no draft, so a node that reads `$draft` is unknown and a gist is offered unless its `when` is false. A goal's payload holds `when` in place of `checks`; `GoalReading` carries the `trace`, each node with `holds` and a `line` node with the line that met it and the check as matched, and `met` is the root's `holds`. A goal that can no longer hold writes one warning, `{ path }`, naming the node that made it so, and `keeps-goals` gives one verdict per goal with that `path`. Law kinds are judged with the lines and the schema too. `collect-writer { key }` joins `BASE_KIT`. The scenario player leaves references in the line. The old shapes are gone: `when: { checks, in, values }`, a goal's `checks`, `while: { key: value }`, `validChecks` and a warning's `{ check: i }`. The three cases are rewritten (SIBAR's «open and an assignee» is a named guard; the review approves with `collect-writer` and no `stamp`, revises a draft or after changes, lets a goal ask for changes, and holds the reviewers while in review once a review was asked), and «published» has one goal on the version on the table: its steps that revoked and rewrote the goal are gone. The five misfits fit, each with its test, and [misfits.md](docs/misfits.md) says so. In `consequitur-render`, `keyOfCheck(goal, path)` and `lineFacts`' warnings read the trace by path, and a goal composed as an `all` keeps its keys. New package, `consequitur-kdl`: `fromKdl` reads a schema written in KDL into the schema object, through `kdlet-guards/kdl` and `@bgotink/kdl`, with problems at their line and column, and `toKdl` writes one back; each case has `schema.kdl`, read to the same object as `schema.json`. The playground's composer sends an `all` of `line` nodes, the dock, the goal's card and «Riga» draw the trace as a tree, each domain has words for its guards, and «Schemi» and «Leggi» write the schema in KDL or JSON. 429 tests in `consequitur` (from 394), 24 in `consequitur-kdl`, 69 in `consequitur-render`, 28 in the playground (from 26).

Round 6: order and scenarios. Goals fire in thread order and a post runs to its end. Each round of `settle` answers every handle denunciation and every warning, and the outcome of one goal, the one whose line comes first in reading order; the others wait for the next round, so a chain of goals settles in one post, and two goals waiting on the same line fire in the order they were set, whatever their names. The bound is derived and counted once: the goals in force that have not written their outcome, plus two; past it the thread pauses as before and `settles` refuses the line, and only a handle kind of a kit that does not cure what it answers can reach it. `MAX_ROUNDS` is gone, and the two tests of the cap became tests of the bound (a chain of three goals and a chain of four settle, two goals on one line, a toy handle kind that never cures). The scenario player moved from `cases/scenario.ts` into the package (`Step`, `Scenario`, `Labels`, `Io`, `Played`, `resolve`, `draftOf`, `playScenario`, `readScenarios`), with a new option, `as`, the writer every step is posted as. `prune(lines, reading, schema)` says, for each line of a thread, whether the engine, a revoke, the world or a goal's check will bring it back, and leaves the rest open for a person to decide on; `stepsOf(kept, all)` turns the lines kept into steps, every id in a payload made a `$line`, a `$goal` or a `$find`. The warehouse is the third case, `cases/warehouse-arrival/` (an arrival registered, announced, unloaded, sorted and shelved): its scenario «Tuesday» is the activity as it happened, «Saved» is what a person saves from it, and the tests prune «Tuesday», check `stepsOf` against «Saved», and play «Saved» as the manager with the lines it awaits fed in, which ends with no difference in the handles. Holes are not built. 394 tests in `consequitur` (from 383), 69 in `consequitur-render`, 26 in the playground.

The reading is `consequitur`. The function that gives a reading, `reduce`, is renamed `consequitur(lines, handles, schema)`: it says what follows, which is what the library is named for, and `reduce` said a fold. Its module is `reading.ts`. The word «reduction» leaves the documents; «reading» names the act and the result. No alias is kept.

New in `consequitur-render`: `changes(before, after)`, what a host animates or tells a person when a reading becomes another, pure and without DOM. It lists a line newly in force or newly revoked, a goal set, met, met no more or gone, a check that found its line, lost it or became an orphan, a denunciation raised or answered, and the thread's settling flipping, typed as `ReadingChange`, ordered by kind and then by key, and empty between two identical readings. `keyOf(denunciation)` and `keyOfCheck(goal, index)` give the two things a reading holds without an id a key that stays the same across reductions, for `changes` and for a host that memoises by reference or keeps nodes with `keyed`. The spec's section 5 says it, [architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) lists `changes.ts` and the glossary's **change** says what a change is between two readings. 383 tests in `consequitur`, 69 in `consequitur-render` (from 53), 26 in the playground.

Plain bugs, fixed, one commit each. `admit` refuses a draft that is not an object whose writer is a string (`writer-not-allowed`) and a payload that is not a plain object (`invalid-payload`), where `post` used to throw or store it; `stamp` stores the payload as JSON holds it, so a line still verifies after a JSON round trip. `stamp` takes `previous` and the latest timestamp from the last line in reading order, never from the array's order. A line the engine writes itself, by a writer of kin `goal` or of a record gist (`warning` included), is neither offered nor admitted (`writer-not-allowed`); `post` used to admit a forged record, and a forged warning kept the real one from being written. A record gist with rules is a problem of the schema (a record states nothing): such a schema used to read without problems and then refuse every post by the law `settles`. New: `readPolicy(json, schema)`, the policy's reader, beside the schema's: it never throws, checks every key at every level, and reads a policy with any problem as the empty one; the playground reads SIBAR's policy with it, and `defineDomain` refuses a policy with problems as it does a schema (a mistyped `while` used to be ignored, and quits on a closed activity answered). A revoke of a line already out of force is refused (`not-revocable`), as it was never offered; a test walks every state the five scenarios pass through and holds the offers and admission to one answer. The reading, the offers and `lineFacts` no longer throw on a stored line whose payload is not an object, which the engine never writes but foreign storage may hold: such a line states nothing. `readSchema` reads a schema with any problem as the empty schema, every problem still listed; it used to leave out the gist or law that failed and use the rest, so a one-word typo in one gist made the next unrelated post erase what that gist had stated. `readGists` and `readLaws` still read their lists element by element. `admit` judges the payload as `stamp` will store it, after a JSON round trip, and refuses one that JSON cannot write or does not give back as an object (`invalid-payload`): a required key that held `undefined` used to be admitted and stored without it, and a payload that held a BigInt or itself made `post` throw. The reading, the offers and `lineFacts` no longer throw on a stored line whose writer is not a string either: such a line names no kin, no id and no goal, where `lineFacts` threw and the reading would have wherever it asks a writer's kin or id (a check that names a kin, a revoke of such a line, `leave`). `kinOf` and `idOf` moved to `util.ts`, still exported, and the engine and `lineFacts` read every writer through them. The `Policy` type says what `readPolicy` accepts: a `while` holds `values`, which the type left optional. `scenarios.test.ts` reads SIBAR's policy with `readPolicy` against the schema and fails on a problem, where one of its tests cast the file. A `now` that does not parse as a date is the caller's mistake and not a refusal: `post`, `dryRun`, `settle` and `stamp` reject with a `RangeError`, as they did, and a test and the engine's spec now say so. In `consequitur-render`, a drag whose start leaves the page ends with null: the band used to clear itself and forget that a drag held it, so a hover took it and a second drag was accepted, and one drop answered both. `dispose` during a drag ends it with null before it removes the drawing; it used to leave the drag's four window listeners on and its promise unanswered for good. After its run rejects, the previewer runs the same draft again when it is given again; it used to keep the key and answer nothing for good. `keyed` keeps a rebuilt node before it removes the old one, so an update entered again from the removal (a blur) no longer leaves an orphan node on the page. In the playground, «Cambia» writes the new goal before it revokes the old: a refusal of the new goal used to leave the old one revoked and the draft taking no goal's place, so the wrapper went on awaiting a revoked goal. «Scrivo come» is disabled while lines are on their way, and a change of it then is refused, as «Azzera» and «Dominio» are: the later lines of «Cambia» used to be written as whoever had been chosen meanwhile. «Dominio» is disabled while lines are on their way too: it refused a change then, but did not say so. While a goal is composed, a card that offers nothing to await has no `awaited`, so a card that had no action either keeps its node: every card used to be built again. When the element pointing at a line (a pill, a tick) is built again, which fires no leave or blur, the page draws the band again, so it clears itself, and the line is lit no more. The dock's blocks are no longer drawn again at the start and the end of every post: their key held whether a post was in flight, which nothing they draw reads. A scroll of the dock draws the band again, as a scroll of the ledger does. The composer's text is read-only while its line is on its way and no longer disabled too, which took its focus. The playground's page is tested under jsdom, driven by clicks (`playground/test/page.test.ts`). 383 tests in `consequitur` (from 340), 53 in `consequitur-render` (from 48), 26 in the playground (from 18).

Handle kinds (`value`, `set`), rule kinds (`value`, `join`, `leave`) and law kinds (`keeps-set`, `keeps-goals`, `settles`) are registered in a `Kit`, `BASE_KIT` holds the base ones, and a schema names the kinds it uses; revoke and goal stay mechanics. `readSchema(json, kit)` reads the whole schema: its head (`opening`, `authority`, `sets`, `records`, `judged`), its gists and its laws, and never throws.
